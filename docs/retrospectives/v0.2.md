# AlifeBattle v0.2 Retrospective

## Result

- The normal battle size was increased from 10 vs 10 to 50 vs 50.
- The v0.1 architecture continued to work without a major redesign.
- A 500-vs-500 battle was used as a performance stress test.
- FPS monitoring and a repeatable simulation benchmark were added.
- A spatial grid was introduced to optimize separation neighbor searches.
- Automated profiling was added to identify current performance bottlenecks.

## Performance

Latest v0.2 benchmark on the development machine:

| Team size | Total agents | Simulation FPS |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50 | 100 | 529.6 |
| 100 | 200 | 177.7 |
| 250 | 500 | 32.4 |
| 500 | 1000 | 9.1 |

The exact values are machine-dependent and are used only for relative comparison.

For the 500-vs-500 profile, target selection dominated the simulation cost:

- `Game.update()`: approximately 52.6 s cumulative
- `_select_targets()`: approximately 47.6 s cumulative
- `find_nearest_enemy()`: approximately 47.5 s cumulative
- `calculate_separation()`: approximately 2.2 s cumulative

## What We Learned

- Local interactions such as separation scale poorly when every agent checks every other agent.
- A spatial grid is effective for local-neighbor queries.
- After separation was optimized, global nearest-enemy targeting became the dominant bottleneck.
- Optimizing measured bottlenecks before introducing more complex architecture worked well.
- The global nearest-enemy movement model does not match the intended long-term battle model.

## Next Direction

Movement and combat targeting should be separated.

Agents should eventually move according to high-level commands and search only locally for enemies that can be attacked.

Therefore, further optimization of global nearest-enemy targeting is deferred.

v0.3 will begin the transition toward command-driven movement.
